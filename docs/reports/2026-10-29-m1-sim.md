# 2026-10-29: M1 hopping link on the simulated ether

`sim/m1_link.py`: the real `air` and `vrxd` programs (built as `air_sim`,
`vrxd_sim` against `sim/radio_sim.c`, UDP multicast on loopback, packets
delayed by their FLRC airtime and dropped per receiver) run in real time on
the laptop. Receivers start 1 to 2 s after the air unit, at a random point of
the hop sequence, like a VRX switched on in the field. FLRC timing constants
are still estimates; this tests the software, not the radio.

| Mode | Loss per RX | First lock (8 runs x 2 RX) | PER per channel (beacons) |
|---|---|---|---|
| LR 520k, 10 ch, 1 beacon/hop | 20 % | 7 to 136 ms | 17.6 to 23.5 % |
| HQ 2.6M, 40 ch, 2 beacons/hop | 10 % | 3 to 259 ms | 8.8 to 10.9 % |

M1 gate (lock < 1 s, stays locked, PER per channel logged and consistent
with the channel): **PASS** in both modes, also in CI (`ctest -L sim`).

Before the mid-hop beacon, one HQ receiver took 1018 ms: a parked receiver
sees its channel once per park window (up to 790 ms with 40 channels), and a
lost beacon cost a whole extra window. The second beacon costs ~3 % of HQ
airtime and turns that risk from p into p^2.

The air unit logs "overruns" (a packet finishing after its hop ended): 2 to
9 per run on a desktop without `--rt`. Measure on the RV1103 with `--rt`.

Run it:

```sh
python3 sim/m1_link.py build build/m2_lr520/reference.ivf --mode hq2600 --loss 0.1
```
